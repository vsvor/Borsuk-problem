# Symmetric bodies: normalization, bounds, and exact constructions

All comparisons use **four** cells and physical Euclidean lengths. The default
normalization is **inradius 1/2**: every facet plane is distance 1/2 from the
origin. In particular this does not mean that the target diameter is 1.
All numerical searches used 2,000 starts per target, seed 23 and four workers,
followed by tighter local polishing. The tetrahedron and octahedron also use an
explicit regular-tetrahedral score construction when it improves the rounded
numerical record. All numerical results are finite-search upper bounds.

## 1. The 6-TRD reference barrier

Put t=1/sqrt(2). The ideal body is

    P6 = {|x|, |y|, |z| <= 1/2,
          |x|+|y|, |x|+|z|, |y|+|z| <= t}.

It has 32 vertices, 18 facets, volume `7 - (9/2)*sqrt(2)`, diameter
`sqrt(3/2)`, and minimum width 1. The vertices are the eight points
`(+/-t/2,+/-t/2,+/-t/2)` and the 24 permutations with one coordinate
`+/-1/2` and the other two `+/-(t-1/2)`.

Define beta4(P) as the infimum of max_i diam(C_i) over four-set covers of P.
Convexity of the cells does not change this infimum for convex P: replacing
C_i by conv(P intersect C_i) cannot increase diameter and preserves coverage.
If P subset Q, clipping a cover of Q proves beta4(P)<=beta4(Q).

Consequently, **conditional on a case body containing a congruent copy of P6**,
any maximum-over-cases bound must be at least beta4(P6). This is the precise
threshold interpretation in the axis-by-axis balancing proposal. No new
universal-containment theorem for arbitrary unit-diameter sets is asserted.

The new result proves only

    beta4(P6) <= 0.963551242461420.

The displayed number is a rigorously rounded-up bound for an explicitly padded
cover. It is NOT a proved lower bound on the method. A global lower bound would
be needed to identify the method's exact barrier.

The optimized unpadded numerical value is 0.9635512424574932. In the saved
53-point numbering, its active pairs (within 1e-8) are the edges of the four
triangles

    (34,35,51), (37,41,47), (39,42,49), (44,48,50).

Each triangle lies on the same three target-edge types as the three-point
minimax problem in `supplementary/truncation_comparison/triangle_certificate.py`,
up to signed coordinate permutations. This explains the repeated numerical
value in the retained 4-, 5-, and 6-truncation patterns. It does not exclude a
cover with a different membership/incidence pattern.

## 2. Ideal Platonic targets

Let phi=(1+sqrt(5))/2. All raw coordinates below are centered at the origin.
`cyclic` means the three cyclic coordinate permutations, with independent signs
on the two nonzero entries.

| Solid | Raw vertices | Scale to inradius 1/2 |
|---|---|---|
| Tetrahedron | (+/-1,+/-1,+/-1), sign product +1 | sqrt(3)/2 |
| Cube | (+/-1,+/-1,+/-1) | 1/2 |
| Octahedron | +/- coordinate unit vectors | sqrt(3)/2 |
| Icosahedron | cyclic (0,+/-1,+/-phi) | sqrt(3)/(2*phi^2) |
| Dodecahedron | (+/-1)^3 and cyclic (0,+/-phi,+/-1/phi) | sqrt(phi+2)/(2*phi^2) |

The icosahedral raw facet normals are the listed raw dodecahedral vectors,
with right-hand side phi^2. Their norm is sqrt(3).
The dodecahedral raw facet normals are the listed raw icosahedral vectors,
again with right-hand side phi^2, and their norm is sqrt(phi+2).
These identities give the scales directly.

The four centrally symmetric Platonic solids have minimum width 2r=1. For a
regular tetrahedron with inradius 1/2, however:

    vertex-to-opposite-face altitude = 2,
    minimum width = sqrt(3),
    edge length = sqrt(6).

Thus the tetrahedral covering bound 3/2 becomes **3/4** for unit altitude, or
**sqrt(3)/2** for unit minimum width. The CLI `--normalization min-width` uses the
latter normalization, while leaving the other solids unchanged. The minimum
width follows also from the difference body of the tetrahedron: its coordinate
support planes are at distance sqrt(3), and its other facets are farther away.

## 3. Independent exact arithmetic for the numerical covers

`ideal_geometry()` in `scripts/symmetric_polyhedra.py` evaluates the formulas
with rational intervals whose square roots are bounded using integer square
roots. It does not call floating-point sqrt for certification.

For an ideal facet n.x<=h choose a rational approximation q to n, and let M be
a rational upper bound on every absolute coordinate of an ideal target point.
The rational inequality

    q.x <= h_upper + M * sum_j max(|q_j-n_j_lower|, |q_j-n_j_upper|)

contains the whole ideal target. Rounding its right-hand side upward gives a
rational outer polytope. This enclosure is regenerated by `tools/verify_results.py`
from the formulas rather than trusting a decimal vertex hull. An inner enclosure
uses the reverse inequality allowance and an explicit coordinate bounding box.

For every saved numerical cell the verifier forms the EXACT rational convex
hull of its decimal coordinates. Padding replaces each facet n.x<=h by

    n.x <= h + 10^-12*||n||_1.

This changes the cover; it is not an arithmetic tolerance. Diameters of the
new hulls are recomputed exactly. Full 3D inclusion-exclusion over all 15
nonempty cell subsets gives **exactly zero** missing volume for all six cases.
Closedness of the hull union implies that zero missing volume proves coverage
of the full-dimensional compact convex target, including its boundary.
The padded hulls may overlap. Clipping them to the ideal target preserves
convexity, coverage and the diameter bound.

## 4. Three elementary exact constructions

### Tetrahedron: beta4 = 3/2

Assign each point to a vertex of maximum dot product (the usual nearest-vertex
Voronoi partition for equal-radius vertices). In raw coordinates the scores
are the four tetrahedral vertices (+/-1)^3 with sign product +1. Every cell is
a polytope with vertices among the original vertices, edge midpoints, face
centroids and the origin. Exact enumeration gives raw squared diameter 3.
After the scale sqrt(3)/2 the squared diameter is 9/4.

For the lower bound, the four original vertices have mutual distance sqrt(6).
A four-set cover of maximum diameter below 3/2 must put them into four distinct
cells. The origin is in at least one cell, and its distance to the vertex in
that cell is exactly 3/2. This is a contradiction. The argument applies to
arbitrary covering sets, not only convex ones.

### Cube: beta4 <= sqrt(3/2)

Split the unit cube at x=0 and y=0. The four boxes have side lengths
1/2,1/2,1 and diameter sqrt(3/2). This construction is certified exactly.
No proof of global optimality for the cube is claimed here.

### Octahedron: beta4 = sqrt(3/2)

In raw coordinates the target is |x|+|y|+|z|<=1. Use the same four tetrahedral
score vectors as above. Each cell contains three non-antipodal axial vertices,
some triangular-face centroids and the origin. The raw squared diameter is
exactly 2. After scale sqrt(3)/2 it is 3/2.

For the lower bound, six octahedral vertices must be covered by four sets, so
one set contains two vertices. Every pair of distinct vertices is at least
sqrt(3/2) apart. This proves the matching global lower bound.

The program checks the raw rational cell vertices, diameters, full union volume,
and absence of pairwise interior overlap for these three constructions.

## 5. An exact icosahedral cover with diameter 3(sqrt(5)-1)/4

The numerical result simplifies to an elementary barycentric construction.
Use raw icosahedral vertices in lexicographic order (as generated by
`scripts/platonic_exact.py`). Assign these twelve vertices to four cells:

    C0: [9,10,11]
    C1: [1,3,7]
    C2: [2,5,8]
    C3: [0,4,6].

Each triple is one original triangular face; the four triples are disjoint.
Add the midpoint of each edge whose endpoints have different owners, to both
owner cells. On every triangular face having three distinct owners, add the
face centroid to all three cells. Add the origin to all four cells. Take each
cell's convex hull. This uses 35 points in total: 12 original vertices,
18 edge midpoints, four face centroids, and the origin.

Boundary coverage is independent of the numerical optimizer. A target face
with one owner lies in that cell. A face with two owners splits into a corner
triangle and a quadrilateral at the two crossing-edge midpoints. A face with
three owners splits into three quadrilaterals using the centroid and the three
edge midpoints. The exact script verifies that these convex patches lie in
their owner hulls, that their pairwise interior areas vanish, and that their
areas sum to the whole target face. The common origin then proves full coverage
by convexity.

All raw coordinates are in Q(sqrt(5)). Exact pairwise distance evaluation gives

    maximum raw squared diameter = 3*phi^2,
    physical scale squared = 3/(4*phi^4),
    maximum physical squared diameter = 9/(4*phi^2) = (27-9*sqrt(5))/8,
    maximum physical diameter = 3/(2*phi) = 3*(sqrt(5)-1)/4
                              = 0.927050983124842... .

This is an exact cover of the ideal icosahedron, **without padding**. It is not
claimed to be globally optimal. No assumption of interior-disjointness of the
four final convex hulls is needed. The boundary patches themselves are disjoint
up to shared boundaries, as the exact script verifies.

A small covering diameter for this comparison body is not, by itself, a better
universal Borsuk bound. Containment of every unit-diameter set in a congruent
copy of the target is a separate requirement, not established here.

## 6. Reproduction

From the repository root:

```bash
python scripts/symmetric_polyhedra.py --run --starts 2000 --workers 4 \
  --seed 23 --seconds-per-start 5 --output runs/symmetric
python tools/polish_symmetric_results.py --input runs/symmetric \
  --output runs/symmetric_polished
python tools/verify_results.py --only six_trd tetrahedron cube octahedron \
  dodecahedron icosahedron --full --overlaps
python scripts/platonic_exact.py --output runs/platonic_exact --export-displays
```

The archived experiments used 600 starts followed by a resumed invocation of
1,400 additional starts. Parallel scheduling, mutations selected from the
current elite pool, and numerical-library versions can change which starts are
explored and the representative coordinates. The checked-in final certificates
are independently reproducible and do not depend on reproducing the searches.
To reproduce the same scheduling protocol as closely as possible, run 600 then
1,400 with `--resume`; bit-for-bit stochastic-search reproducibility is not
promised.

For newly generated results, the standalone verifier regenerates the ideal target:

```bash
python scripts/verify_symmetric_cover.py runs/symmetric/six_trd/best.txt \
  --solid six_trd --pad 1e-12 --diameter-bound 0.964 --full-volume \
  --report runs/new_six_trd_check.json
```

The default pad is an explicit change of cells, not a numerical tolerance. Use
`--pad 0` to check the raw decimal hulls. Decimal files exported from a symbolic
construction are for display only; refer to the exact JSON and its reconstruction
script for the unpadded mathematical cover.
